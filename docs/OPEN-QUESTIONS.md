# River corrections, round 2 (lines, areas, reasons)

### Alpine Rhine (round 2)

- The natural course in the 1850 overview is traced on the Dufour map. It may be 50–100 m off in places, and the gravel area also takes in some banks beside the bed.
- The Fussach cut line is today's course from OpenStreetMap, cut at the 1909 shore.
- Lengths from the IRR: the Fussach cut is about 5 km of new bed and made the river 7 km shorter; the Diepoldsau cut made it about 3 km shorter.
- Fussach dates: HLS says 1896–1900, IRR says 1895–1900. The site uses 1895–1900.
- No source confirmed the floods of 1834 and 1871, so they are left out.
- Markers placed only approximately: the 1927 dam break at Schaan, the Ill mouth, and Haag.

### Eastern rivers (round 2)

- Ticino: works 1888–1939 (HLS), with drainage in steps from 1918 to 1961. No source confirms "canal ready by 1912".
- Muota: 1911–1926 is not confirmed. A Federal Court ruling of 1931 says only "after 1910".
- Thur: zh.ch dates the 5 km canal at the mouth to about 1860, which is earlier than the HLS project of 1874–1893.
- Traces on the Dufour-type maps (Ticino 1858, Seez 1854, Melchaa 1864/1870, Thur 1870) are accurate to about 100–200 m.
- Outlines read by eye: the Glatt marsh at Rümlang/Kloten, the Flachsee, and the Maggia delta.
- Reuss in Uri: no source shows the old delta before the canal.

### Western rivers (round 2)

- Linth: the long old course comes from the 1811 plan, placed on today's map. It can be 100–400 m off and is named "approximate". From general knowledge, not checked in a source: the decision of the Tagsatzung in 1804, the financing by shares, the name "Escher von der Linth", and the renewal since 2008.
- Kander: the last 2 km of the old course to the Aare are estimated. The gravel area on the Allmend is drawn by hand.
- Areas drawn by hand: the Rhône delta and the bed at Martigny, the Grosses Moos, the Orbe marsh, the Emme and the Saane.
- Emme: the 1870 map already shows dams, so the old bed area is uncertain.
- Hasli Aare: the 1864 map already shows a straight river above Brienzwiler. Only the loop near the lake is drawn.
- Jura water correction: the lake lowering of about 2.5 m comes from secondary sources (2.5 m to almost 3 m). La Nicca's 1842 plan and the 1867 federal decree are not checked in a source.
- Rhône: the farmers' protests against the third correction are not checked in a source.
- The "Why" texts for the Saane, Areuse, Gürbe and Emme rest mostly on the general HLS article and the federal laws of 1876/1877.

# Gained and lost (round 4)

Every "gained" and "lost" item has a short quote from a source in `research/evidence-gainlost.json`. Items marked "region" there use a source for the wider valley, not only this stretch.

- No item yet: Thur in the Toggenburg (no gains), Suze in the Vallon and Areuse (no gains and no losses). No source says what these works protected or what they destroyed.
- No safety gain stated in a source: Seyon, Gürbe, Seez, Orbe, Broye, Reuss in Uri, Ticino, Aire, Aare at Klingnau, Aare at Gösgen, Vedeggio, Chur to Tardis. Sources often say that floods continued after the works.
- Malaria: sources say malaria was a problem in the Linth plain, the Rhône valley, the Orbe plain, the Magadino plain and the Fully plain. No source says that a correction ended it. Malaria left Switzerland about 1900 in general (Vischer, BWG 2003).
- Lungerersee: HLS gives a lowering of 18 m and 170 ha of new land; Vischer gives 35 m and 90 ha. The site uses HLS.
- Linth: the losses (marshes, fevers) rest on one newspaper interview (Südostschweiz 2023).
- Limmat (Dietikon) and Töss: all items rest on one source each (local yearbook; cantonal media release 2018).
- Hinterrhein in the Domleschg: the items rest on one parliamentary question (Graubünden 2006).
- Seymaz: the 1998 bill blames later floods on growing towns and modern farming, not on the lost marsh. The "why" text is changed to say this.
- Muota: the flow problems that the canton names come mostly from the power plants of the 1950s/60s, not only from the correction.
- Kander: one lost item is about the later works (from 1899) on the same river, not the 1714 cut.
- Lost species are named only where a source names them (lake trout of Lake Thun, salmon in the Limmat, nase in the Sense with unclear cause).
