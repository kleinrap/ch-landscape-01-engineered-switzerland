What we are not sure about

This site is built from careful research, but not every fact is certain. Below is everything we marked as uncertain. If you know more, or can check an archive, please tell us.

Facts to check: Engineered Switzerland

The research agents marked these as uncertain. Items with no online source can be checked in archives and libraries (see docs/VISITS.md once written).

River corrections

  • HLS articles disagree on some dates and lengths: Linth 1807–1816 or 1807–1823, and 15 km or 17 km; Rhône (first correction) 1863–1884 or 1863–1894; Rhône (second correction) 1930–1960 (HLS) or 1936–1961 (canton); Hasli Aare 1866–1875 or 1866–1880; Gürbe works until 1911; Ticino works until 1939. The site uses the HLS table.
  • Areuse: the table gives 14 km for Travers–Couvet. This looks too long.
  • Hagneck canal: the length (8 km), the opening (1878) and the lake lowering (2.5 m to almost 3 m) come from secondary sources.
  • Lengths not from a source: Reuss in Uri "c. 4.5" (measured on the map), Seez "c. 12". The Reuss plain, Maggia and Melchaa have no length.
  • Little detail was found for the Nozon (Orny), the Muota (1911–1926) and the Saane (1917–1987).
  • The Reuss in Uri has no "before" map, because the first map (1864) already shows the canal.
  • The Kander's old course is drawn only in the old valley (Glütschbach). The part across the Thun Allmend is left out.
  • A few markers are placed only approximately (Haag, Oberglatt, Glattbrugg, the airport, Pfungen, Brunnen, the mouths of the Gürbe and the Melchaa).
  • Some statements are general knowledge, not from the sources: the Port weir and the Meienried reserve (Zihl), the Bolle di Magadino (Ticino), the airfield on the Maggia delta, and "the vegetable region" (Jura). The Rhône "until about 2040" comes from a news summary.

River corrections, round 2 (lines, areas, reasons)

Alpine Rhine (round 2)

  • The natural course in the 1850 overview is traced on the Dufour map. It may be 50–100 m off in places, and the gravel area also takes in some banks beside the bed.
  • The Fussach cut line is today's course from OpenStreetMap, cut at the 1909 shore.
  • Lengths from the IRR: the Fussach cut is about 5 km of new bed and made the river 7 km shorter; the Diepoldsau cut made it about 3 km shorter.
  • Fussach dates: HLS says 1896–1900, IRR says 1895–1900. The site uses 1895–1900.
  • No source confirmed the floods of 1834 and 1871, so they are left out.
  • Markers placed only approximately: the 1927 dam break at Schaan, the Ill mouth, and Haag.

Eastern rivers (round 2)

  • Ticino: works 1888–1939 (HLS), with drainage in steps from 1918 to 1961. No source confirms "canal ready by 1912".
  • Muota: 1911–1926 is not confirmed. A Federal Court ruling of 1931 says only "after 1910".
  • Thur: zh.ch dates the 5 km canal at the mouth to about 1860, which is earlier than the HLS project of 1874–1893.
  • Traces on the Dufour-type maps (Ticino 1858, Seez 1854, Melchaa 1864/1870, Thur 1870) are accurate to about 100–200 m.
  • Outlines read by eye: the Glatt marsh at Rümlang/Kloten, the Flachsee, and the Maggia delta.
  • Reuss in Uri: no source shows the old delta before the canal.

Western rivers (round 2)

  • Linth: the long old course comes from the 1811 plan, placed on today's map. It can be 100–400 m off and is named "approximate". From general knowledge, not checked in a source: the decision of the Tagsatzung in 1804, the financing by shares, the name "Escher von der Linth", and the renewal since 2008.
  • Kander: the last 2 km of the old course to the Aare are estimated. The gravel area on the Allmend is drawn by hand.
  • Areas drawn by hand: the Rhône delta and the bed at Martigny, the Grosses Moos, the Orbe marsh, the Emme and the Saane.
  • Emme: the 1870 map already shows dams, so the old bed area is uncertain.
  • Hasli Aare: the 1864 map already shows a straight river above Brienzwiler. Only the loop near the lake is drawn.
  • Jura water correction: the lake lowering of about 2.5 m comes from secondary sources (2.5 m to almost 3 m). La Nicca's 1842 plan and the 1867 federal decree are not checked in a source.
  • Rhône: the farmers' protests against the third correction are not checked in a source.
  • The "Why" texts for the Saane, Areuse, Gürbe and Emme rest mostly on the general HLS article and the federal laws of 1876/1877.

Regional river corrections (round 3)

Regional corrections, east

  • Hinterrhein in the Domleschg: HLS gives 1832 as the start, the HLS Realta article gives 1830. The 1859 map already shows the new east dam.
  • Chur to Tardis bridge: "1868" in the years is the flood year, not a confirmed start. La Nicca's exact role is unclear.
  • Rheintal inland canal: 1895–1906 (HLS) or 1894–1906 (canal association).
  • Thur in the Toggenburg: 1907–1913 (HLS) or 1907–1914 (St. Gallen state archive).
  • Landquart: that the 1910 flood led to the 1912–1918 canal is the agent's own reading. The widening sites are placed roughly.
  • Inn and Flaz at Samedan: HLS gives no exact years for the Inn correction. The canton gives two different costs.
  • Vedeggio: only the start year 1906 is known.
  • The marsh outlines in the Werdenberg and the Rheintal are rough.

Regional corrections, centre

  • Lorze in the Baar plain: the only date is "1970s" (HLS). The link to the N4a motorway is not checked in a primary source.
  • Wägitalersee: no start year was found, only "dam finished 1924".
  • Limmat (Zurich–Dietikon): the main source is a local yearbook (Jahrheft Schlieren 2002). Klingnau and Bünz rest on canton of Aargau flyers. "1886" for Klingnau is the start of the canalisation, not of one single project.
  • Bünz: the old line covers only Wohlen to Dottikon.
  • Lungerersee and Sihlsee are lake projects (lowering and damming), not river corrections in the strict sense.
  • The Limmat and Lorze new lines come from swisstopo swissTLM3D, not OpenStreetMap.
  • Lengths given as "c." are estimates.

Regional corrections, west

  • Seyon (Neuchâtel): 1843 (HLS and canton). No map is older than the work, so the red line follows Rue de l'Écluse and Rue du Seyon. It is labelled "approximate".
  • Sense: the canton of Bern says only "around 1900". No exact years and no length.
  • Aare Thun–Bern: which stretch belongs to which correction (1824–1859, 1871–1892) is read from a sketch. Lines are drawn only for Thun–Uttigen. The length "c. 25 km" is an estimate.
  • Broye: the oldest usable map (1870) already shows the 1853–1856 canal, so that step has only a blue line.
  • Fully–Saillon: 1910–1940 comes from the title of a study that the agent did not read. The building year of the Canal de la Fontaine is not confirmed. The marsh outline is drawn by eye.
  • Suze (Vallon de Saint-Imier): HLS gives 1910–1940 for "a good third" of the river, without naming the stretches. Linking Courtelary–Cortébert to it is the agent's reading of the maps. The reasons in "Why" are general.
  • Seymaz: the marsh areas are drawn by eye.

Gained and lost (round 4)

Every "gained" and "lost" item has a short quote from a source in research/evidence-gainlost.json. Items marked "region" there use a source for the wider valley, not only this stretch.

  • No item yet: Thur in the Toggenburg (no gains), Suze in the Vallon and Areuse (no gains and no losses). No source says what these works protected or what they destroyed.
  • No safety gain stated in a source: Seyon, Gürbe, Seez, Orbe, Broye, Reuss in Uri, Ticino, Aire, Aare at Klingnau, Aare at Gösgen, Vedeggio, Chur to Tardis. Sources often say that floods continued after the works.
  • Malaria: sources say malaria was a problem in the Linth plain, the Rhône valley, the Orbe plain, the Magadino plain and the Fully plain. No source says that a correction ended it. Malaria left Switzerland about 1900 in general (Vischer, BWG 2003).
  • Lungerersee: HLS gives a lowering of 18 m and 170 ha of new land; Vischer gives 35 m and 90 ha. The site uses HLS.
  • Linth: the losses (marshes, fevers) rest on one newspaper interview (Südostschweiz 2023).
  • Limmat (Dietikon) and Töss: all items rest on one source each (local yearbook; cantonal media release 2018).
  • Hinterrhein in the Domleschg: the items rest on one parliamentary question (Graubünden 2006).
  • Seymaz: the 1998 bill blames later floods on growing towns and modern farming, not on the lost marsh. The "why" text is changed to say this.
  • Muota: the flow problems that the canton names come mostly from the power plants of the 1950s/60s, not only from the correction.
  • Kander: one lost item is about the later works (from 1899) on the same river, not the 1714 cut.
  • Lost species are named only where a source names them (lake trout of Lake Thun, salmon in the Limmat, nase in the Sense with unclear cause).
